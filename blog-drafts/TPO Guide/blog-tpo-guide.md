# What is TPO in Gel Nails? Why It's Being Removed from Professional Formulas

Until recently, most nail professionals had never heard of TPO. That is changing fast.

Trimethylbenzoyl Diphenylphosphine Oxide — TPO — is one of the photoinitiators responsible for curing gel polish under UV and LED lamps. It has been a standard component of gel nail formulations for years. It is efficient, inexpensive, and widely compatible with color systems.

It is also the subject of growing regulatory attention in the EU, documented health concerns, and a wave of reformulations by professional brands that saw the direction of travel early.

This guide explains what TPO is, what the concerns are, where EU regulation currently stands, and what "TPO-free" actually means for professional practice.

---

## What is TPO?

TPO stands for Trimethylbenzoyl Diphenylphosphine Oxide. Its full INCI name is Diphenyl(2,4,6-trimethylbenzoyl)phosphine oxide. It belongs to the acylphosphine oxide class of photoinitiators.

Its function in gel nail products is to initiate polymerization. When UV or LED light hits a TPO molecule, it absorbs the energy and generates reactive free radicals. Those radicals trigger the chain reaction that cures the gel — converting it from a liquid monomer system into a solid, cross-linked polymer.

TPO is particularly efficient under LED lamps, which is one reason it became prevalent as the industry moved from UV to LED curing systems over the past decade. From a pure formulation-performance standpoint, TPO works.

The question the EU regulatory process has been examining is what else it does.

## Why TPO is Being Removed from Professional Formulas

### The Health Concerns

The primary documented concern with TPO is photoallergy and skin sensitization.

Photoallergy is a specific type of immune-mediated reaction in which the sensitizing molecule requires UV or visible light exposure to trigger the response. In practical terms, this means skin contact with a TPO-containing product — followed by UV/LED light exposure during the curing process — can sensitize an individual. Once sensitized, subsequent exposure to TPO under light triggers an allergic reaction.

TPO has also been identified as a contact sensitizer independent of photoactivation, though the photoallergic pathway is the primary mechanism of concern in the gel nail context.

The combination of skin contact during application, proximity to the UV/LED source, and the frequency of repeated service appointments creates a sensitization exposure profile broadly similar to the HEMA problem the industry has been addressing. The physiological consequence — once an individual is sensitized — is the same: permanent immune memory and ongoing reactivity to the allergen.

### The EU Regulatory Position

The EU's Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety (SCCS) has assessed photoinitiators used in nail products, including acylphosphine oxide compounds in the TPO family. The direction of those assessments, combined with the EU Cosmetics Regulation's precautionary approach to skin sensitizers, has placed TPO on a regulatory trajectory toward restriction or removal from cosmetic products.

Under EU Cosmetics Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, substances that are identified as skin sensitizers or that pose unacceptable risk to consumer health based on SCCS opinion can be added to Annex II (prohibited substances) or Annex III (restricted substances) through the standard committee process.

Professional brands formulating for the EU market that tracked the SCCS opinions and the Annex amendment process had advance notice that TPO's regulatory status was under active review. Those that acted on that information — reformulating before a formal ban took effect — are now positioned on the right side of the compliance curve.

## The Industry Shift

The nail industry's response to TPO concerns has followed a pattern familiar from the HEMA debate: early awareness in professional and regulatory circles, gradual publication of safety concerns, and then a compression of reformulation timelines as regulatory clarity increased.

Professional brands at the quality end of the market began removing TPO ahead of any mandated deadline. The rationale was straightforward: why carry a liability — for clients, for technicians, for brand reputation — when the technical capability to reformulate exists?

Alternative photoinitiators, including bisacylphosphine oxides and phenylbis(2,4,6-trimethylbenzoyl)phosphine oxide variants, as well as entirely different photoinitiator classes, are available to formulators. A TPO-free gel that cures correctly under standard LED lamps is achievable. The reformulation cost is real but not prohibitive for any manufacturer operating with serious quality systems.

The market signal is also clear. Salons and distributors that understand ingredient safety are actively asking about TPO-free status. Being able to answer that question with documented confirmation — not a vague "we comply with regulations" — is a commercial differentiator.

## What TPO-Free Means for Professional Practice

### Safer Services

Removing TPO from the formulation eliminates a documented sensitization risk from every service you perform. That is the baseline benefit. Your clients are not accumulating exposure to a photoallergen. Your technicians are not accumulating occupational sensitization risk from day-in, day-out application.

### Future-Proof Product Selection

Salons investing in TPO-free ranges today are not going to face a mandatory product switch when EU regulatory restrictions formally take effect. The transition costs — training, inventory management, client communication — are absorbed now, on your own timeline, rather than under pressure when a deadline arrives.

### Compliance Confidence

A product that is TPO-free, HEMA-free, and CPNP-notified under EU Cosmetics Regulation gives you a documented compliance position. If a client or a regulatory body ever asks about your product safety, you have the answer and the paperwork to back it.

## The Combined Risk of HEMA and TPO

HEMA and TPO are separate molecules with separate sensitization pathways, but they co-occur in a large proportion of standard gel polish systems on the market.

A formula that removes HEMA but retains TPO has addressed one sensitization risk while leaving another in place. A formula that removes TPO but retains HEMA has done the same in reverse. A truly safe professional formula — one that a salon can defend to clients, to regulators, and to its own professional standards — must address both.

The reason is cumulative sensitization burden. A client may not be sensitized to either HEMA or TPO individually at a given point in time. But their immune system is accumulating exposure to multiple sensitizing compounds through every service. Removing one while retaining the other is a partial solution.

The professional standard is increasingly both-free. That is where the EU regulatory trajectory points, where informed professional buyers are moving, and where formulation science has made it feasible to operate.

## What to Look For When Switching to TPO-Free

Not every brand's "TPO-free" claim is equally verifiable. Here is what to require before switching:

### CPNP Notification

All products sold as cosmetics in the EU must be CPNP-notified. A notified product has a Cosmetic Product Safety Report on file. Ask for the CPNP notification reference for every product in the system.

### EU Formulation and Manufacturing

A product formulated and manufactured in the EU is subject to EU regulatory oversight at the point of production. Products imported from outside the EU are required to comply with EU cosmetic regulations at the point of sale — but the manufacturing process was not under EU oversight. For a professional product with safety claims, EU origin matters.

### Full INCI Declarations

TPO appears on INCI ingredient lists as **Diphenyl(2,4,6-trimethylbenzoyl)phosphine oxide**. Related compounds in the acylphosphine oxide family — such as phenylbis(2,4,6-trimethylbenzoyl)phosphine oxide (BAPO) — are different molecules. Confirm that what is absent is specifically TPO, and confirm it for every layer in the system.

### Independent Certifications

Leaping Bunny Approved status requires independent verification of cruelty-free claims throughout the supply chain. ISO compliance documentation demonstrates quality management system standards. These do not directly address TPO status, but a brand that has obtained independent certifications has demonstrated a willingness to have its claims externally verified — a relevant signal for ingredient safety claims generally.

### A Direct Answer

Ask your supplier directly: is this product TPO-free? If the answer is not an immediate, documented yes — with supporting documentation — treat that as an answer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is TPO currently banned in the EU?

As of the date of this publication, TPO has not been formally added to the EU Cosmetics Regulation's Annex II (prohibited substances). However, SCCS assessments and the regulatory process have put it under active scrutiny, and the direction of EU cosmetic ingredient policy regarding skin sensitizers has been consistently toward restriction. Professional brands reformulating now are acting on regulatory trajectory, not just current status.

### Does removing TPO affect gel cure quality?

In a well-formulated TPO-free system, no. Gel cure quality depends on photoinitiator selection and concentration, lamp wavelength compatibility, and formulation balance. Modern TPO-free gel systems from quality manufacturers cure completely under standard LED lamps with no perceptible difference in durability, hardness, or flexibility compared to TPO-containing systems.

### Why has TPO received less attention than HEMA if it carries similar risks?

HEMA received attention first because methacrylate sensitization has a longer documented clinical history in the nail industry and a clearer established mechanism in occupational dermatology literature. TPO's sensitization pathway is more recently documented and the regulatory action has developed more slowly. The term "TPO-free" itself barely existed as a professional search term before late 2025 — the professional market's awareness is now accelerating rapidly. Brands positioned on this issue now are ahead of where client and distributor demand will be within the next product cycle.
